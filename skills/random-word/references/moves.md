# Moves for connecting the word to the focus

Use these as a toolbox: try a different move in each chain. The examples are original, not from the book.

## 1. Associative chain
From the word jump to a concept close to it, and from that concept to another, until you touch the focus. The intermediate stepping stones do the work.

Focus: *onboarding for an app.* Word: **beehive**.
beehive — worker bees get tasks by age — newcomers start with simple tasks near the entrance → new users get one minimal guided task before seeing every feature.

## 2. Attribute transfer
Pick a property of the word and carry it over to the focus.

Focus: *a weekly team meeting.* Word: **ice**.
ice — melts over time — the meeting opens with a 5-minute "thaw" for off-agenda topics, which runs out by itself.

## 3. Wordplay
Exploit double meanings, sound or alternative senses.

Focus: *a clothing store.* Word: **button**.
button — panic button / buy button / sample button → a "sample button" on each garment: an express fitting with no commitment.

## 4. Antithesis
Use the opposite of the word or of its function.

Focus: *an instruction manual.* Word: **padlock**.
padlock — closes — the opposite: opening → a manual that "unlocks" in layers, from basic to advanced.

## 5. Physical object
Look at the real object: shape, weight, texture, parts, what it lacks. An object offers more angles than its name because we tend to think about its function; here the point is to think about what it is.

Focus: *learning a language.* Object: **a sponge**.
sponge — absorbs, has pores, gets squeezed — learn by absorbing (immersion), then "squeeze out" what you learned in a short daily conversation.

## 6. New entry point
Ask yourself: which part of the focus had I not looked at that this word makes me see?

## 7. Disconnected jump (PO)
Write an idea without explaining how you got there and fill the gap later: `PO …`. Useful when a chain gets stuck.

## Signs a chain is going well
- An idea appears that you would not have proposed without the word.
- There is a surprising stepping stone between the word and the focus.
- The final idea is concrete (it can be tested or drawn).

## Signs it is time to let go and move on
- You are forcing a "reasonable" connection.
- More than 5 minutes have gone into a single chain.
- You are repeating an idea already stated in other words.
